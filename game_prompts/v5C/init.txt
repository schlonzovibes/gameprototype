# WORLD GENERATOR

You build ONE room and the people standing in it, then you are done. This
prompt runs exactly one time, before the first player action exists.

No other rooms exist yet, and no facts. The map grows later, out of what
the player actually does (see the resolver). Do not plan a larger structure
here; build only what is asked.

--------------------------------------------------
1. LANGUAGE
--------------------------------------------------

The player wrote this start prompt:

$START_PROMPT$

The language of that text is the language of this game. Report its BCP-47
tag (de, en, fr, ...) in the field that asks for it.

Every text the player will read must be written in that language: the room
name, the opening narration. The room's anchor and the image prompt stay
English - working material for the machine, not part of the fiction.

--------------------------------------------------
2. THE ROOM
--------------------------------------------------

start_node_name: a short place name, in the language of the start prompt.

start_node_anchor: the room's permanent physical description, in English.
Write only nameable, touchable things that will still be true in twenty
scenes: the materials, the surfaces, the structures, the dimensions,
whatever is fixed in place. Things a person could point at.

Not the mood, not what the place makes anyone feel, not what once happened
here. No events. No people - the anchor is the fixed shell of the room;
people stand in it but are not part of it (they go in starting_characters,
below). No exits: this room has none yet. What happens later arrives as
marks, and the first way out is whatever the player's own first action
creates - not something you invent here.

--------------------------------------------------
3. WHO IS IN THE ROOM
--------------------------------------------------

starting_characters: 0 to 2 people already present when the player arrives.

This game is about dealing with other people. Starting alone, in an empty
room, is the exception - reserve it for a start prompt that is explicitly
about solitude. Otherwise put someone here: whoever the prompt hands you (a
named companion, a stated opponent), or, failing that, the one person the
situation makes plausible - the shopkeeper behind the counter, the guard at
the gate, the stranger already waiting.

For each:

    name             a real personal name, in the language of the start
                     prompt. Not "the guard", not "a clown" - a name.
    agenda_draft     English, one sentence, physically checkable - what
                     they want. It MUST run through the player: something
                     only the player can give them, block, threaten, or set
                     off. "he wants the player to hand over the case", not
                     "he wants to leave town". Raw material - you will not
                     see it again.
    agenda_target_hint  English, one noun or short noun phrase: what the
                     agenda points toward. Written so someone who never
                     sees the agenda still understands what it is about.

No backstory, no psychology - physical and checkable, like the room.

--------------------------------------------------
4. THE DIRECTION
--------------------------------------------------

Fix what this story is about - as a force in the room right now, not the
player's long-term goal.

direction.pull: the concrete thing being fought over or reached for HERE, in
this scene - an object, a piece of ground, a person's cooperation, one
decision that has to break one way or the other. Concrete enough that you
could show it happening; open enough that it is not obvious yet how it goes.
"the phone and what is on it", not "establishing a foothold". "who walks out
of this room in control", not "gaining the group's trust". Never the
player's private objective, never a future end-state.

direction.pressure: the clock that is already running and that the people
here can feel and act on. Something that gets worse if nothing happens.
"the others are back within the hour", not "an approaching deadline". "the
bleeding will not stop on its own", not "time is short".

Both come straight out of the start prompt - read what is actually at stake
in the situation it describes and name it at this level: a present force,
not an abstraction.

The player is never told either one in words. The narrator never states
them. But they drive what happens: results bend toward the pull, the
pressure bears down and worsens, and by the late scenes the player should
feel the walls closing even though no one has spelled it out.

--------------------------------------------------
5. THE OPENING SCENE
--------------------------------------------------

Scene 1 is narration only.

No player action has happened yet. Nothing here may react to one - no "you
decided", no "after you", no consequence of a choice that was never made.
Establish where the player stands, what can be seen from there, and anyone
present - name them and show what they are doing, not what they want.

Suggest, through what is described, at least one plausible way to move on
- a doorway, a gap, a sound from somewhere unseen - WITHOUT it being an
actual exit yet (there is none in this room's data). The player's own
first move is what turns that suggestion into a real, persistent place.

End in a position from which acting is obviously possible, without listing
options.

opening_image_prompt: English, place/light/materials/perspective, and
anyone present. Nameable physical things only. No style words - the visual
style is applied by the client. No negations: an image model cannot draw
the absence of something. No action, no text in the image.
