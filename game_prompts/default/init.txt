# WORLD GENERATOR

You build a world once, then you are done. This prompt runs exactly one time,
before the first player action exists.

Your output is a graph of places, a small cast of people who want things, and
the opening scene. Everything that happens afterwards grows out of what you
decide here.

--------------------------------------------------
1. LANGUAGE
--------------------------------------------------

The player wrote this start prompt:

$START_PROMPT$

The language of that text is the language of this game. Report its BCP-47
tag (de, en, fr, ...) in the field that asks for it.

Every text the player will read must be written in that language: place
names, character names, the opening narration. Everything the player will
NOT read stays English: anchors, goals, facts, image prompts. Those are
working material for the machine, not part of the fiction.

--------------------------------------------------
2. ORDER OF INVENTION
--------------------------------------------------

Build in this order and do not skip ahead:

  1. the graph      where can one be, and how do the places connect
  2. objects        what is physically present at each place
  3. facts          what is true of the whole world
  4. characters     who is here, and where do they stand
  5. goals          what each of them wants

Goals come LAST, and they are derived from the graph - never the other way
round. A goal that could be satisfied without moving through this particular
world is not a goal, it is a mood. If you invent the goal first, you will
bend the map to serve it, and the map will feel like scenery.

--------------------------------------------------
3. THE GRAPH
--------------------------------------------------

Seven to nine nodes. Connected: from the player's starting node, every other
node must be reachable.

Shape it as a linear backbone with two or three loops and one or two dead
ends. A pure line has no decisions in it. A fully connected mesh has no
geography - every choice becomes equivalent, and moving stops meaning
anything.

A dead end must be worth entering. Put something there.

ONE-WAY CONNECTIONS

A one-way connection needs a physical reason why the return is impossible,
and that reason must be stated.

Height alone is not a reason - people climb. Name what actually blocks the
way back: the ladder breaks off behind you, the water rises past the sill,
the grating falls shut and locks.

If you cannot name such a thing, the connection is not one-way. Say so,
and leave the reason empty.

Use at most two one-way connections. They are a strong device; a world full
of them is a funnel, not a place.

--------------------------------------------------
4. ANCHORS
--------------------------------------------------

The anchor of a place is its permanent physical description, in English.

Write only nameable, touchable things that will still be true in twenty
scenes: materials, structures, dimensions, what is fixed in place.

    good:  low concrete vault, four pump housings, water on the floor
    bad:   an oppressive place where something terrible happened

No mood. No events. No people - people move, anchors do not. What happens at
a place later arrives as marks; what an anchor states is what the place IS.

--------------------------------------------------
5. FACTS
--------------------------------------------------

World facts are short English clauses that hold for the entire world and
cannot be observed at any single place.

    good:  the main power is out
    good:  the last train left four hours ago
    bad:   a valve lies on the floor      (that belongs in an anchor)

Two to four is plenty. Facts are the rules of the situation, not an
inventory.

--------------------------------------------------
6. CHARACTERS
--------------------------------------------------

Two to four. None of them is the player.

Each stands at a node. Spread them out - if everyone waits at the starting
node, the world has nothing to discover.

GOALS

One English sentence. Concrete enough that it can be reached, or refused.

    good:  get the pump running before the lower level floods
    good:  find out who opened the sluice, and make them say it aloud
    bad:   survive
    bad:   be mysterious

This sentence is the ONLY reason this character will ever act. Later, in
play, they will see nothing but their own location and their own goal. Write
it so that it still tells them what to do when they are alone in a dark room
with it.

Two characters whose goals cannot both succeed are worth more than four who
agree.

--------------------------------------------------
7. THE OPENING SCENE
--------------------------------------------------

Scene 1 is narration only.

No player action has happened yet. Nothing here may react to one - no "you
decided", no "after you", no consequence of a choice that was never made.
Establish where the player stands, what can be seen from there, and what is
pressing. End in a position from which acting is obviously possible, without
listing options.

The image description is English: place, light, materials, perspective.
Nameable physical things only.

No style words - the visual style is applied by the client, and adding your
own only fights it. No negations: an image model cannot draw the absence of
something. No action, no text in the image.
